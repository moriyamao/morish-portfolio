Deep Learning-Based Segmentation and Classification of Breast Lesions in Ultrasound Datasets.

Academic research prototype / external validation pending

From an image to a region.

This team study investigates a pipeline that predicts lesion categories, bounding boxes, and segmentation masks from breast ultrasound images. The work compares small and nano YOLO26 segmentation models and examines how augmentation, dataset size, and training configuration affect the results.

The paper combines BUSI, BUS-UCLM, and BUS-BRA into a balanced set of 1,374 original images, with 458 images in each reported category: Normal, Benign, and Malignant. The principal augmented configuration contains approximately 3,200 images. Augmented copies do not represent additional independent patients.

More images did not mean better boundaries.

The reported experiments distinguish broad localization at an IoU threshold of 0.50 from stricter localization across thresholds of 0.50–0.95. Larger augmented datasets improved some mAP@50 results while reducing performance under the stricter metric. The nano model was competitive at mAP@50; the small model performed better at mAP@50:95 in the reported model-size comparison.

For the paper's principal YOLO26s configuration: box mAP@50 0.842, box mAP@50:95 0.597, mask mAP@50 0.838, and mask mAP@50:95 0.557. Rounded values are reported from the manuscript, not independently reproduced here. These metrics are not diagnostic accuracy.

The team also examines confusion matrices and class-specific precision-recall curves, separating category confusion from background-related detection errors.

What the results can support.

This is a co-authored academic study, presented as an experimental research prototype. It does not establish clinical effectiveness or readiness for patient use. A hosted Streamlit prototype is available. The manuscript reports that dedicated external-dataset evaluation and CPU-based ONNX deployment were unfinished at the time of writing; a hosted interface alone does not establish either milestone.

A reproducibility review should also document patient-level split membership, augmentation lineage, how the Normal category is annotated for instance segmentation, and the exact checkpoints used for evaluation. Those details matter when interpreting a score from a mixed-source dataset.

The downloadable manuscript is the supplied academic document; no peer-reviewed publication or IEEE acceptance is claimed.
