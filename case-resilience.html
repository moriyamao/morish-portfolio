Preparing for
the interruption.

Capital and Storage Constrained Resilience: A Scenario-Based Agent-Based Simulation of Supplier Diversification and Inventory Buffering Strategies in the Philippine Food Manufacturing Context.

Agent-based modelingMonte CarloLatin Hypercube SamplingRandom Forest

Read the paper (PDF) ↗

Preliminary academic demonstration / co-authored research

Two strategies. Limited resources.

A business can prepare for a supply interruption by keeping additional inventory or by arranging alternative suppliers. Both choices use money, and stored inventory also uses space. This study models how those constraints interact with disruption duration and severity.

The simulation compares strategies under shared scenario parameters and uses repeated stochastic runs to estimate service levels. A Random Forest is used to examine patterns in the simulation outputs. This is a mechanism-testing model situated in a Philippine manufacturing context.

A demonstration before the full study.

The manuscript's demonstration uses 100 scenarios and 20 Monte Carlo repetitions per strategy. It reports that buffering was preferred in 85 scenarios, diversification in 12, and three fell within the study's equivalence tolerance. Disruption duration was the strongest reported Random Forest predictor.

These are preliminary, manuscript-reported simulation outcomes, not independently reproduced results or measured outcomes from operating businesses. The planned full design is 1,000 scenarios with 100 repetitions.

What still needs resolving.

The manuscript records unresolved parameter sourcing, partially paired random streams, an unused supplier-count parameter, and a model without revenue replenishment. Its methodology and limitations also differ on the calibration dataset and the use of STL and SHAP. The limitations report broader manufacturing data and substituted methods.

A reproducible final version should reconcile those descriptions, document each parameter source, and complete the intended experiment before presenting the directional findings as established results.

Shared work.

Co-authored by Aaron John D. Gonzales and Morish Alfonso Macayan, School of Information Technology, Mapúa University.
